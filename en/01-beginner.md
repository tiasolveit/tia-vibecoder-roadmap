# 🟢 Track 1: Vibecoder Beginner

**Goal:** Build and launch your first working version.

From nothing to a URL someone can visit. Nothing in this track is about
polish or scale. It is about proving the idea exists outside your head.

Do the steps in order. Each one takes minutes, not days. If you feel stuck,
that is usually a sign you skipped a step.

---

## 1. 🧠 The Idea

Write down one problem you understand better than anyone else, and who has it.

**Done when:** You can describe the problem in one sentence and name the person
who has it in one word. Example: "Freelancers lose invoices in email, and they
are the client."

---

## 2. 🤖 AI Suggestions

Turn your problem into ten possible products, then pick the one you would
actually use.

Use the 4-part formula from [00-prompt-basics.md](00-prompt-basics.md). Give
context about you and your constraints, not just the idea.

**Done when:** You have a shortlist of three ideas and you picked one.

---

## 3. 💡 More Ideas

Expand the idea. Find adjacent problems, edge cases, and the boring features
people forget until it is too late.

**Done when:** You have a feature list of at least 20 items, split into
must-have and nice-to-have.

---

## 4. 📂 Organization

Set up the repo, a notes file, and a task list. Decide where you will keep
prompts and decisions.

Suggested layout: one folder per step area, a `NOTES.md` for thinking, a
`TODO.md` for tasks.

**Done when:** Everything you have thought so far is written down somewhere you
will actually look at again.

---

## 5. 📣 Market Validation

Before you write code, check that anyone wants this. Talk to five people who
have the problem. Ask what they do today, not whether they like your idea.

Best case is someone who already pays for a bad workaround. That is a real
market signal.

**Done when:** At least five conversations happened and two of them ended with
someone asking when it will be ready.

---

## 6. ⚙️ Tech Stack

Pick the boring stack you already know. Do not learn three new frameworks to
build one small product.

Choose for speed of shipping and cost, not for how it looks on your resume.

**Done when:** You can say the stack out loud in one sentence, and you know the
monthly cost before you deploy.

---

## 7. 🎯 MVP Definition

Cut the feature list down until it is embarrassing. The MVP is the smallest
thing that delivers the value from step 1.

If it needs a login to be useful, it needs a login. If it needs a mobile app,
drop it.

**Done when:** You can describe the MVP in five bullet points or fewer, and
every point is required for the product to work.

---

## 8. 🧩 Build Plan

Break the MVP into tasks of a few hours each. Put them in order. Put the
riskiest assumption first, because if it fails you want to know in day two.

**Done when:** You have an ordered task list and it fits in a realistic number
of days.

---

## 9. ✅ Review

Read the plan once more with fresh eyes. Cut anything that does not serve the
MVP. Write prompts for the first three tasks while you are still here, because
context is fresh.

**Done when:** The task list changed and got shorter, and your first prompts are
written.

---

## 10. 🚀 Build

Build. One task at a time. Do not rewrite working code to look nicer. Do not
add a feature that was not on the list.

When AI gets stuck, give it the actual error text and the actual file, not a
description of the problem.

**Done when:** The whole flow works end to end on your machine.

---

## 11. 🌐 Launch & Test

Deploy it, get a public URL, and hand it to five real people. Watch them use
it without explaining anything.

Do not guide them. Silence is the most useful feedback you will ever get.

**Done when:** A real person outside your team has used it and told you what
confused them.

---

You have a live product and real feedback. Move to the growth track.
